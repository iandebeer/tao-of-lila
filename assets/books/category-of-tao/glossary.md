# Working Glossary

## Category theory and mathematics

### Category
A mathematical structure consisting of objects and morphisms between them, together with identity morphisms and associative composition.

**In this book:** a disciplined way of shifting attention from isolated things toward relationships and transformations.

---

### Morphism
An arrow

\[
f:A\rightarrow B
\]

representing a structure-appropriate relationship or transformation.

**In this book:** crossing, Way, movement from noun toward verb.

**Not:** Tao itself.

---

### Composition
Given

\[
A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{g}C,
\]

the morphisms compose:

\[
g\circ f:A\rightarrow C.
\]

**In this book:** ways joining to become ways; streams becoming rivers.

---

### Identity morphism
For every object \(A\):

\[
\mathrm{id}_A:A\rightarrow A.
\]

It satisfies:

\[
f\circ\mathrm{id}=f,
\qquad
\mathrm{id}\circ f=f.
\]

**In this book:** an important image for participation without alteration and later for questions of identity, consciousness and wu wei.

**Not:** consciousness itself.

---

### Isomorphism
A morphism possessing an inverse.

\[
A\cong B.
\]

**In this book:** the distinction between literal identity and structural sameness; associated with return.

---

### Monoid
A structure with an associative binary operation and an identity element \(I\).

\[
a\otimes I=I\otimes a=a.
\]

**In this book:** accumulation and the discovery of that which adds nothing.

---

### Idempotence
An operation or element is idempotent when repetition produces no further change:

\[
e\otimes e=e.
\]

The identity of a monoid is necessarily idempotent.

**In this book:** *enough*.

---

### Product / Coproduct
Dual categorical constructions characterized by universal properties.

**In this book:** ways of approaching the One and many without reducing either to mere aggregation.

---

### Universal property
A way of characterizing an object by its uniquely determined relationships rather than its internal construction.

**In this book:** one of the first major steps toward relational rather than substantial description.

---

### Opposite category
\(\mathcal C^{op}\) is obtained by reversing all morphisms in \(\mathcal C\).

**In this book:** relational reversal, arising/returning, and a possible disciplined conversation with yin and yang.

---

### Functor
A structure-preserving mapping between categories.

**In this book:** translation between worlds without claiming those worlds are identical.

---

### Natural transformation
A coherent transformation between functors.

**In this book:** transformation of perspective while preserving relational coherence.

---

### Monad
A categorical structure involving an endofunctor together with unit and multiplication satisfying coherence laws.

**In this book:** potentially a language for contextual/effectful transformation.

---

### Kleisli arrow
For a monad \(T\), a Kleisli arrow has the form:

\[
A\rightarrow T(B).
\]

**In this book:** action whose result occurs within effect, condition or context.

---

### Comonad
The categorical dual of a monad, with counit and comultiplication.

**In this book:** a possible language for situatedness and dependence upon context.

---

### Adjunction
A relationship between functors:

\[
F\dashv G
\]

expressed through a natural correspondence between morphism sets.

**In this book:** complementary directions that fit together without becoming identical.

---

### Yoneda lemma
A central theorem of category theory relating an object to the natural transformations from/to its representable Hom-functor. Informally, the complete relational behaviour of an object determines it up to isomorphism.

**In this book:** the categorical culmination of the movement from substance toward relationship.

**Not:** a proof of Taoism, emptiness or non-duality.

---

## Taoist terms

### Dao / Tao — 道
Way.

The central term of the *Tao Te Ching*, deliberately resistant to exhaustive definition.

**In this book:** never identified with a morphism, category, object or theorem. Category theory is used to reduce unnecessary predicates around Tao, not to capture it.

---

### De — 德
Virtue, potency, efficacy or the particular expression of Dao.

Especially important in Chapter 51: Dao gives rise; De nourishes.

---

### Mother
A recurring Laoist metaphor for the generative source of the ten thousand things.

Especially important in Chapters 1, 25, 34 and 52.

**In this book:** explored relationally but not equated with an initial object or other categorical construction.

---

### Ten thousand things — 萬物
The multiplicity of manifest things.

**In this book:** a natural image for multiplicity understood not merely as a collection of objects but through relationships among them.

---

### Master / Sage — 聖人 (*shengren*)
Laozi's sage.

**In this book:** increasingly characterized not by accumulated predicates but through relationship to Tao and the ten thousand things.

---

### Ziran — 自然
Literally something close to “self-so”; naturalness, spontaneity, being so of itself.

**In this book:** important to the movement from prescribed process toward unforced action.

---

### Wu wei — 無為
Literally “non-doing/non-action,” but inadequately understood as simple inactivity.

**In this book:** non-forcing, non-coercive participation; the philosophical culmination toward which the book moves.

---

## Buddhist and Vedantic terms

### Śūnyatā — Emptiness
The Mahāyāna Buddhist teaching of emptiness of inherent, independent existence.

**In this book:** relationality without independent self-subsistence.

**Not:** zero, nothingness, the empty set or a categorical initial object.

---

### Form
Especially through the *Heart Sutra*:

**Form is emptiness; emptiness is form.**

Form is not annihilated by emptiness.

**In this book:** the object remains; what is challenged is the assumption that it stands independently.

---

### Gate gate pāragate pārasaṃgate bodhi svāhā
The concluding mantra of the *Heart Sutra*, evoking going, going beyond and the farther shore.

**In this book:** introduced through the river crossing and the morphism.

---

### Neti neti — नेति नेति
“Not this, not this”; often rendered “not this, not that.”

An Upanishadic method of refusing to identify ultimate Self/reality with any finite predicate.

**In this book:** subtraction of accumulated identities.

---

### Sat–Chit–Ānanda
Being–Consciousness–Bliss.

A Vedantic formulation associated particularly with Advaita.

**In this book:** not three properties accumulated by the self, but a contemplative counterpoint to the diminishing of predicates.

---

### Hari Om Tat Sat
A devotional/contemplative expression incorporating *Om Tat Sat*, which appears in the *Bhagavad Gita*.

**In this book:** *Tat* — “That” — resonates with pointing rather than defining; *Sat* with being/reality.

---

## Recurring metaphors

### The Arrow
Relationship, movement, transformation.

The first categorical image of the book.

---

### The River
The Way between this shore and the farther shore.

Also composition: streams join streams and become rivers.

---

### Finger and Moon
The distinction between representation and that which is represented.

Category theory itself remains a finger.

---

### Moon and Water
Transformation of appearance without necessary transformation of what is reflected.

Used to explore invariance, identity and perception.

---

### More
Accumulation.

\[
a\otimes b\otimes c\otimes\cdots
\]

Associated particularly with Chapters 44 and 48.

---

### Enough
The cessation of accumulation.

Associated with idempotence:

\[
E\otimes E=E.
\]

---

### Nothing
A deliberately dangerous word.

It may refer to relinquishment of self-assertion, predicates or possession, but should never casually be equated with mathematical zero, Buddhist emptiness or nonexistence.

The eventual paradox:

**Tao — everything.  
Master — nothing.**

must therefore be developed rather than merely asserted.
