# Chapter 14 — Wu Wei

*The action that does not force*

The book has travelled from knowing to acting.

Chapter 48 gave us the destination long before we understood it:

**Diminishing and diminishing again, one arrives at wu wei.  
Through wu wei, nothing is left undone.**

Now return to the identity morphism:

\[
A\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_A}A.
\]

It was never the absence of a morphism.

It participates.

And under composition:

\[
f\circ\mathrm{id}=f,
\qquad
\mathrm{id}\circ f=f.
\]

It adds no distortion of its own.

Likewise the monoidal identity:

\[
a\otimes I=a.
\]

Again, neither identity *is* wu wei.

But after the journey through the book we can see why these tiny mathematical structures caught our attention.

They offer an image of:

**participation without imposition.**

Chapter 51 supplies the ethical expression:

Give rise without possessing.

Act without claiming.

Nourish without ruling.

And Yoneda has supplied the relational culmination.

The Master does not disappear.

The Master does not become inert.

The Master is fully present in relationship while ceasing to insist upon being its centre.

Wu wei is therefore not:

**nothing happens.**

It is closer to:

**nothing is forced from a separate centre that must impose itself upon what is.**

The Way began as an arrow:

\[
A\longrightarrow B.
\]

We followed it through movement, return, identity, multiplicity, emptiness, context and relationship.

Eventually we discovered that the arrow itself was another finger pointing at the moon.

Perhaps now it too can be relinquished.
