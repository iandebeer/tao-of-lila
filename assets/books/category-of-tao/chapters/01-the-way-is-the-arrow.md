# Chapter 1 — The Way Is the Arrow

*Morphism, relationship, crossing, composition*

The book begins not with the object but with the arrow:

\[
A\longrightarrow B.
\]

*Anderkant nêrens* supplies the experiential opening. The crossing of the river introduces the morphism; *gate gate pāragate pārasaṃgate bodhi svāhā* introduces crossing and the farther shore; the finger pointing at the moon warns us that neither words nor mathematical notation are the thing toward which they point.

Laozi's Chapters 25 and 40 introduce following, movement and return. Water introduces composition and multiple paths.

The chapter plants, but does not yet explain, the question that will eventually become Yoneda:

**Could a thing be known through its relationships?**
