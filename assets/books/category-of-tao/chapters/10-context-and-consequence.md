# Chapter 10 — Context and Consequence

*Monads, Kleisli arrows, comonads*

An ordinary arrow:

\[
A\rightarrow B
\]

may prove inadequate for lived action.

A Kleisli arrow:

\[
A\rightarrow T(B)
\]

introduces effect and context.

Action never occurs in an empty universe.

Every act enters conditions already present.

Comonadic thinking adds the complementary question:

**Can the thing be separated from the context in which it exists?**

Tree and soil.

Person and community.

Organism and environment.

Self and world.

This brings relationality increasingly close to the practical question of *wu wei*.
