# Chapter 13 — The Mother and the Ten Thousand Things

*Chapters 51 and 52*

Chapter 51:

Dao gives rise.

De nourishes.

Things take form.

Circumstances complete them.

Yet Dao gives rise without possessing.

Acts without claiming.

Nourishes without ruling.

Rather than representing Tao naively as one more object:

\[
T\rightarrow X_1,\quad
T\rightarrow X_2,\quad
T\rightarrow X_3,
\]

we begin asking whether that representation already says too much.

If Tao is merely another object among objects,

\[
\{T,X_1,X_2,\ldots\},
\]

we have turned Tao into another one of the ten thousand things.

Chapter 52 deepens the problem.

There is Mother.

There are children.

Knowing Mother, one knows the children.

Knowing the children, one returns to Mother.

The relational skeleton is:

\[
M\rightarrow X
\]

and:

\[
X\rightarrow M.
\]

But Mother need not be identified with a categorical object.

The question becomes:

> **Could the source be disclosed through the pattern of relationships without ever becoming another object within that pattern?**

Chapter 52 also introduces the opposite direction:

\[
M\rightarrow X
\qquad\leftrightarrow\qquad
X\rightarrow M.
\]

Arising.

Returning.

Not necessarily inverse morphisms.

Not necessarily isomorphism.

Simply a reversal of attention.

And eventually even the nouns can be removed:

\[
\bullet\longrightarrow\bullet
\]

then:

\[
\longrightarrow
\]

Relation remains.

Category theory has not defined Tao.

It has merely helped us remove some of the words.
